Our remit

Three disciplines, one shared challenge

SIDM focuses on the practical contribution of standards, interoperability and data management to safe, coherent and effective digital health. These areas overlap and should not be treated as separate silos.

01 / Standards

Choosing, applying and improving standards

Clinical coding and terminology, clinical information models, technical and professional standards, conformance, lifecycle governance and the evidence that a standard meets an actual need.

Questions include what a specification is for, who maintains it, how it connects with related standards, and how implementers can use it effectively.

02 / Interoperability

Working across boundaries

Semantic and technical interoperability, information exchange, business processes, shared workflows, architecture and the responsibilities required for coherent cross-organisational use of information.

Interoperability should be assessed through real outcomes rather than the simple presence of an interface or common data format.

03 / Data management

Keeping information meaningful and trustworthy

Metadata, common definitions, identifiers, reference data, data quality, stewardship, catalogues and the operational functions needed to govern and maintain data as a reusable resource.

Data management is a continuing capability, not an incidental by-product of system implementation.

Cross-cutting questions

Where these disciplines meet

Several recurring challenges are worth exploring across organisational and professional boundaries.

Shared meaning

Who defines a data item, where is the authoritative definition maintained, and how can that meaning be reused across different specifications?

Implementation and assurance

How do we distinguish a technically valid specification from one that is usable, interoperable and safe in practice?

Learning and discoverability

How can professionals find a starting point, identify related standards and navigate the communities that maintain them?

Sustainable stewardship

What organisations, responsibilities and processes are needed to maintain standards, metadata and reference data over time?

Suggest a question worth exploring.

Ideas can begin as a public proposal in our community repository. They do not need to arrive as fully formed projects.
