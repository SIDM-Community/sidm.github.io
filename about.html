About the community

A space for people who need standards to work

SIDM stands for Standards, Interoperability and Data Management. We are developing an open, cross-disciplinary community focused on the practical use and improvement of health and care information standards.

Purpose

Information standards can only be effective when they are understood in context, aligned to real needs and supported by sound data management. SIDM aims to bridge the distance between specialist standards communities and the people who design, deliver, manage and use health and care information systems.

Our interest goes beyond exchanging data between applications. It includes the processes that produce information, the definitions that give it meaning, the quality of that information and the organisational arrangements needed to maintain it over time.

Who is it for?

The community seeks contributions from computing professionals, health and care practitioners, informaticians, enterprise and solution architects, data professionals, policy specialists, researchers, suppliers and standards organisations. No single profession or technology should determine the agenda.

How we intend to work

Initial activity centres on shared learning, webinars, working discussions, reference material and proposals for focused collaborative work. Rather than create parallel standards catalogues unnecessarily, the community favours finding and connecting authoritative material that already exists.

The emphasis is on evidence, practical problems, constructive challenge and transparent provenance. Contributions and working proposals do not become institutional policy simply by appearing on the website.

Relationships with other organisations

SIDM has developed through discussions associated with the BCS Faculty of Health and Care and engagement with people active in other standards and professional communities. This site is a community-managed publication and is not an official website of BCS, HL7 UK, PRSB or any other organisation.

Formal relationships, endorsements and governance arrangements should be represented here only when explicitly agreed and authorised.

What happens next?

Early priorities include providing a findable starting point for standards resources, making it easier to propose and discuss work, and establishing a review process for material suitable for public release. The scope and organisation of future work will be refined with participants.
